How Do Search Engines Work?

A search engine helps people find information on the web. It discovers webpages, organises information about them and tries to show useful results when someone enters a search.

1. Crawling: discovering pages

Search engines use automated programs, often called crawlers or bots, to discover webpages. They may follow links from pages they already know about and read publicly accessible site information such as XML sitemaps. A page can be online without being discovered immediately.

2. Indexing: understanding and storing information

After discovering a page, a search engine may analyse its text, images, links and other signals. If the page is suitable, information about it can be stored in a large index. Indexing is not guaranteed: technical problems, duplicate content or quality concerns can affect whether a page is included.

3. Ranking: choosing what to show

When someone searches, the search engine looks through its index and orders results using many signals. These can include how closely a page matches the query, its usefulness, quality, usability, location for local searches and the context of the search. The exact systems are complex and change over time.

How can a website help search engines?

  • Publish useful, original content written for people.
  • Give every important page a clear title and description.
  • Use descriptive links so pages can be discovered and understood.
  • Make the site work well on phones and load reliably.
  • Use a valid XML sitemap to help search engines find important URLs.
  • Check indexing reports and technical issues in the relevant search engine tools.

Does submitting a sitemap guarantee ranking?

No. A sitemap is a discovery aid, not a promise that every URL will be indexed or appear near the top of results. Search engines make their own decisions about crawling, indexing and ranking.

Remember: Search engines search their index of the web; they do not normally scan every website from scratch each time you type a query.
