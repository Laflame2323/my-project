What Is Cloud Computing? A Simple Explanation

Cloud computing means using computing services—such as storage, software or processing power—over the internet instead of relying only on your own device.

How does the cloud work?

When you save a file to an online storage service, the file is sent through the internet to computers in data centres. You can then access it from another supported device by signing in. The provider maintains the underlying servers, networks and software.

The word “cloud” does not mean that your files float somewhere in the sky. It is a simple name for remote computers and services accessed through a network.

Everyday examples

  • Online storage: save documents and photos so you can access them on different devices.
  • Web email: read and send messages through a browser or app.
  • Streaming: watch video or listen to audio delivered over the internet.
  • Online documents: create and edit files in a browser, sometimes with other people at the same time.
  • Website hosting: run a website on computers managed by a hosting provider.

Benefits of cloud computing

Cloud services can make files easier to access, simplify collaboration and reduce the need to maintain your own servers. Many services also let users increase or reduce storage and computing capacity as needs change.

Things to consider

Cloud services often depend on a reliable internet connection. Read the provider's privacy and security information, use a strong unique password, enable multi-factor authentication and keep backup copies of important files. Check free storage limits and subscription costs before relying on a service.

Beginner tip: Before uploading sensitive documents, understand who can access them, how sharing works and how you can download or delete your data.

Key takeaway

Cloud computing lets people use storage, software and computing power through the internet. It is convenient, but users should still protect their accounts and understand the service's limits.
